# De-AI Landing Hero

**Category:** visual
**Source:** https://designmotionhq.com/patterns/de-ai-landing-hero
**Verification:** direct-page

> Your landing page looks AI-made. Five tells. Five fixes.

## Source-derived guidance

Design Motion identifies recurring landing-hero choices that can make a page feel generic: adjective-heavy headlines, decorative gradient blobs, two equally weighted CTAs, unverifiable social proof, and generic feature cards. Its proposed direction is to replace decoration with concrete product information: name the outcome and audience, show the product, establish one primary action, use attributable proof with a measurable result, and annotate real product UI.

## Core principle

A landing hero should communicate a concrete product value proposition before decorative styling. Visual choices should reinforce the product rather than substitute for missing product information.

## Rules

- Make the headline communicate a concrete outcome instead of stacking generic adjectives.
- Make the supporting copy clarify who the product is for when audience context matters.
- Establish a clear primary CTA rather than giving two equal actions the same visual weight.
- Prefer attributable, concrete proof over vague usage numbers or anonymous logo rows.
- When feature communication is needed, prefer real product UI or concrete evidence over generic icon-and-word cards.

## Do

- Show the product when the product itself is the strongest evidence of value.
- Give the primary CTA clear visual priority.
- Use customer evidence with a name, role, and measurable result when such evidence is genuinely available.
- Treat decorative gradients, blobs, and feature-card ornament as optional—not as substitutes for content.

## Don't

- Fill the headline with vague adjectives such as “supercharge,” “seamless,” or “powerful” without a concrete outcome.
- Use decorative gradients as the main source of visual interest when the product could provide stronger evidence.
- Present two competing primary CTAs with equal visual weight without a clear reason.
- Invent social proof, metrics, testimonials, or customer results.

## Agent action

For a landing-hero task, first identify the product, audience, desired outcome, primary action, and available evidence. If the existing hero relies heavily on generic decoration or vague claims, replace the weakest decorative element with concrete product information before adding more visual polish. Preserve existing brand direction when it already communicates the product clearly.
